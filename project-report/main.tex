\section*{Abstract}

This dissertation presents a system for \textbf{generative clinical narrative and risk explanation} from ICU time-series data using large language models, grounded in the \textbf{eICU Collaborative Research Database} (eICU-CRD). The work transforms structured critical-care measurements---vitals, laboratory results, respiratory support, and derived risk scores---into natural-language summaries that improve interpretability and support clinical decision-making.

The implementation loads the PhysioNet \textbf{eICU-CRD demo v2.0.1} (2,500+ ICU stays, SQLite and CSV.gz tables) into \texttt{data/eicu-crd-demo/} and maps real \texttt{patientunitstayid} records to the \texttt{icu\_agents} pipeline. Specialized agents (\texttt{RiskAgent}, \texttt{NarrativeAgent}, with synthetic fallbacks for sparse modalities) produce coherent patient bundles orchestrated by \texttt{PatientPipeline} and \texttt{data/pipeline\_source.py}. Apache Airflow DAGs apply \textbf{rule-based clinical reasoning}---hourly severity labelling, hypoxia/sepsis branching, and coordinator escalation---on eICU-derived vitals and lactate panels. OpenAI LLM integration and agent frameworks (LangChain/LangGraph) are configured for narrative enrichment and future BeeAI deployment.

\chapter{Introduction}

Critical care environments generate high-frequency, multimodal data: vital signs, laboratory results, imaging reports, ventilator settings, and risk assessments. The \textbf{eICU Collaborative Research Database} provides a widely used, de-identified resource for ICU research, while recent large language models (LLMs) enable transformation of structured time-series into readable clinical narratives with temporal reasoning.

This M.Tech final-semester project (4th semester) addresses the gap between raw ICU tables and clinician-facing explanations. The \texttt{icu\_agents} module loads the PhysioNet eICU-CRD demo from local SQLite, maps vitals and laboratories to a shared \texttt{PatientState} schema, and runs rule-based risk scoring plus narrative generation. Apache Airflow DAGs apply hourly severity reasoning and hypoxia/sepsis branching on real time-series, with synthetic fallbacks when charting is sparse. Streamlit and CLI interfaces support stay selection by \texttt{patientunitstayid}. Code is maintained at \url{https://github.com/suryashekhawat/MTech4SEM}.

\section{Problem Statement}

Clinicians and researchers struggle to interpret high-dimensional ICU time-series at the bedside. Structured vitals and labs in relational tables are difficult to scan quickly, while risk scores alone lack narrative context. The problem addressed is to \textbf{transform structured ICU data into natural-language summaries}, apply \textbf{LLMs for clinical narrative generation and risk explanation}, and improve \textbf{interpretability and decision support}---using eICU-CRD where available and a modular multi-agent pipeline for end-to-end prototyping.

\section{Objectives}

The primary objectives of the project are:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item To transform structured ICU time-series from eICU-CRD into natural-language clinical summaries.
    \item To apply large language models for narrative generation, risk explanation, and temporal reasoning.
    \item To build a Streamlit UI with modular pages (summary, global overview, time series, patient report, cohort features).
    \item To design a multi-agent architecture (\texttt{icu\_agents}) for vitals, labs, radiology, respiratory, risk, and narrative outputs.
    \item To integrate Apache Airflow DAGs for batch generation, temporal branching, and artifact persistence.
    \item To improve interpretability and prototype clinical decision-support workflows (in-progress: BeeAI, OpenAI, natural-language queries).
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope of the Project}

The scope includes eICU-CRD demo access (\url{https://physionet.org/content/eicu-crd-demo/2.0.1/}), automated download via \texttt{scripts/download\_eicu\_crd\_demo.py}, SQLite-backed loading in \texttt{icu\_agents/data/eicu\_loader.py}, and dual-mode execution (\texttt{ICU\_DATA\_SOURCE=eicu} or \texttt{synthetic}). Streamlit and CLI entry points support stay selection by \texttt{patientunitstayid}. Airflow DAGs mount the local demo dataset and persist JSON/report/timeline artifacts under \texttt{icu\_agents/output/}. LLM integration is staged for production narrative enrichment. The system is a research prototype and not validated for live clinical deployment.

\chapter{Literature Survey}

Healthcare analytics increasingly relies on synthetic and semi-synthetic data when real cohorts are unavailable. Prior work on ICU scoring (e.g., severity indices) and sepsis detection emphasizes temporal vitals and laboratory markers such as lactate. Workflow engines such as Apache Airflow are widely used for ETL and ML pipelines in industry. Multi-agent and agentic AI frameworks (including LangChain and LangGraph, listed in project dependencies) support decomposed reasoning tasks, though this project's agents are currently implemented as plain Python classes with explicit orchestration.

\section{Existing Systems}

Existing approaches to ICU data simulation include:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Statistical simulators} that sample marginals independently, often breaking cross-field clinical consistency.
    \item \textbf{EHR-based synthetic generators} (e.g., GAN or copula-based) that require source data and substantial training infrastructure.
    \item \textbf{Rule-based clinical engines} embedded in research simulators, which encode domain knowledge but may lack modular orchestration.
    \item \textbf{Workflow-orchestrated pipelines} in production ML platforms, which schedule tasks but may not model clinical agents explicitly.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations of Existing Systems}

Monolithic generators are difficult to extend when new modalities (e.g., radiology narratives) are required. Black-box deep generative models demand large datasets and offer limited interpretability for education and pipeline testing. Systems without orchestration layers complicate scheduling, artifact versioning, and audit trails. Few lightweight prototypes combine interactive UI, batch DAG execution, and temporal branching for escalation logic in a single codebase.

\section{Proposed Solution}

The proposed \texttt{icu\_agents} system addresses these gaps through:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Specialized agents} (\texttt{VitalsAgent}, \texttt{LabAgent}, \texttt{RadiologyAgent}, \texttt{RespiratoryAgent}, \texttt{RiskAgent}, \texttt{NarrativeAgent}) with diagnosis-aware parameters.
    \item \textbf{A unified \texttt{PatientState}} model aggregating all modalities.
    \item \textbf{\texttt{PatientPipeline}} for in-process orchestration and \textbf{Airflow DAGs} for production-style task graphs.
    \item \textbf{Temporal reasoning} with hourly severity labels and branch tasks for respiratory and sepsis escalation paths.
    \item \textbf{Persistent artifacts} (JSON bundles, text reports, timeline traces, dashboard snapshot).
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Proposed Methodology}

This chapter explains the overall methodology, system architecture, data flow, and algorithms used in the \texttt{icu\_agents} implementation.

\section{System Architecture}

The architecture has four layers:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Data:} PhysioNet eICU-CRD demo SQLite (\texttt{eicu\_v2\_0\_1.sqlite3}) and CSV.gz tables under \texttt{data/eicu-crd-demo/}, loaded by \texttt{data/eicu\_loader.py}.
    \item \textbf{Presentation:} \texttt{app.py} (CLI) and \texttt{ui/streamlit\_app.py} (web UI with stay selector).
    \item \textbf{Orchestration:} \texttt{orchestrator/patient\_pipeline.py} and \texttt{data/pipeline\_source.py}; Apache Airflow DAGs for batch and temporal reasoning.
    \item \textbf{Agent layer:} Risk and narrative agents on real measurements; synthetic generators used only when eICU fields are missing.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{eICU data path.} For \texttt{ICU\_DATA\_SOURCE=eicu}, the loader queries \texttt{patient}, \texttt{vitalperiodic}, \texttt{lab}, \texttt{admissiondx}, \texttt{diagnosis}, \texttt{note}, and \texttt{respiratorycharting} for a selected \texttt{patientunitstayid}. Vitals are mapped to hourly traces (heart rate, SpO$_2$, respiratory rate, temperature, systolic BP); labs are normalized to WBC, lactate, creatinine, platelets, and hemoglobin. \texttt{RiskAgent} and \texttt{NarrativeAgent} consume this \texttt{PatientState}; sparse radiology rows fall back to \texttt{RadiologyAgent} templates.

\textbf{Synthetic fallback path.} When \texttt{ICU\_DATA\_SOURCE=synthetic}, \texttt{generate\_base\_patient()} seeds demographics and all modality agents run as before.

Airflow DAGs call \texttt{build\_patient\_dict()} so the same eICU mapping is used in Docker (volume-mounted demo data) and on the host.

\section{Algorithm or Model Description}

\textbf{Base patient generation.} \texttt{generate\_base\_patient()} randomly selects age (24--88), gender (Male/Female), and diagnosis from six conditions.

\textbf{VitalsAgent.} For each hour in a 24-hour window, samples vitals from Gaussian distributions centered on diagnosis-specific baselines (e.g., Sepsis: higher heart rate and temperature, lower SpO$_2$; ARDS: SpO$_2$ baseline 85\%).

\textbf{LabAgent.} Samples WBC, lactate, creatinine, platelets, and hemoglobin with diagnosis-adjusted means (elevated lactate and WBC for Sepsis).

\textbf{RadiologyAgent.} Returns a fixed impression string per diagnosis from a lookup table.

\textbf{RespiratoryAgent.} Sets mechanical ventilation and FiO$_2$/PEEP ranges for ARDS and Sepsis; otherwise room-air defaults.

\textbf{RiskAgent.} Rule-based scoring: adds weighted points if SpO$_2$ $<$ 90, heart rate $>$ 120, lactate $>$ 4, or creatinine $>$ 2; caps mortality risk at 100 and derives correlated sepsis and deterioration scores.

\textbf{Temporal reasoning (Airflow, eICU-backed).} \texttt{build\_hourly\_reasoning\_trace} walks the eICU-derived vitals list and labels each hour as \textit{stable}, \textit{worsening}, or \textit{critical} when SpO$_2$ or heart rate cross clinical thresholds (e.g., SpO$_2$ $<$ 90 and HR $>$ 115 $\Rightarrow$ worsening; SpO$_2$ $<$ 85 or HR $>$ 125 $\Rightarrow$ critical). Each trace row stores an explicit \texttt{reason} string for auditability. \texttt{branch\_hypoxia} and \texttt{branch\_septic\_shock} route to escalation tasks when terminal SpO$_2$ $<$ 85 or lactate $>$ 4~mmol/L from the eICU lab panel. \texttt{coordinator\_agent} merges branch outcomes, proposes FiO$_2$/PEEP adjustments, and emits sepsis action lists; \texttt{generate\_temporal\_narrative} summarizes critical hours and escalation flags for the final report.

\section{Workflow Diagram}

\textbf{Local pipeline (\texttt{PatientPipeline.run}):}
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item Generate base patient $\rightarrow$ create \texttt{PatientState} with UUID.
    \item VitalsAgent $\rightarrow$ LabAgent $\rightarrow$ RadiologyAgent $\rightarrow$ RespiratoryAgent.
    \item RiskAgent $\rightarrow$ NarrativeAgent $\rightarrow$ return patient.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Airflow \texttt{icu\_multi\_agent\_pipeline} (eICU-integrated):}
\texttt{load\_patient\_from\_source} (eICU SQLite $\rightarrow$ risk $\rightarrow$ narrative) $\rightarrow$ \texttt{store\_patient\_bundle} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{update\_dashboard}.

\textbf{Airflow \texttt{icu\_temporal\_reasoning\_pipeline}:}
\texttt{load\_temporal\_bundle} (eICU vitals/labs) $\rightarrow$ \texttt{build\_hourly\_reasoning\_trace} $\rightarrow$ parallel branch tasks (hypoxia, sepsis) $\rightarrow$ \texttt{coordinator\_agent} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{optional\_langgraph\_handoff} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{generate\_temporal\_narrative} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{store\_temporal\_outputs}.

\section{Development Environment}

Development was carried out in Python using a virtual environment (\texttt{python -m venv .venv}). The package root is \texttt{icu\_agents/}. Local execution:
\begin{verbatim}
cd icu_agents
pip install -r requirements.txt
python app.py
streamlit run ui/streamlit_app.py
\end{verbatim}
Airflow is deployed via \texttt{docker-compose.yml} (PostgreSQL, webserver on port 8080, scheduler) with DAGs mounted from \texttt{airflow/dags/} and the eICU demo volume \texttt{../data/eicu-crd-demo}. Project configuration in \texttt{config.py} defines \texttt{DATA\_SOURCE} (default \texttt{eicu}), \texttt{EICU\_DEMO\_DIR}, \texttt{OPENAI\_MODEL}, \texttt{MAX\_ICU\_HOURS = 72}, and \texttt{SEED = 42}. Download the dataset with \texttt{scripts/download\_eicu\_crd\_demo.sh} before first run.

\section{Dataset Description}

\textbf{eICU Collaborative Research Database (demo v2.0.1).} The primary data source is the open PhysioNet demo (\url{https://physionet.org/content/eicu-crd-demo/2.0.1/}), comprising 2,500+ de-identified ICU stays from 20 hospitals. Files are stored locally under \texttt{data/eicu-crd-demo/} (git-ignored) and include gzipped CSV tables plus \texttt{sqlite/eicu\_v2\_0\_1.sqlite3}. The loader maps \texttt{vitalperiodic} rows to hourly traces, latest \texttt{lab} values to the agent lab schema, \texttt{note} text to radiology reports when available, and \texttt{respiratorycharting} FiO$_2$/PEEP to ventilation fields.

\textbf{Agent bundles (eICU + synthetic fallback).} Each bundle includes:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Demographics: \texttt{patient\_id}, \texttt{age}, \texttt{gender}, \texttt{diagnosis}.
    \item \texttt{vitals}: list of 24 hourly dictionaries (timestamp, heart\_rate, temperature, spo2, resp\_rate, systolic\_bp).
    \item \texttt{labs}: WBC, Lactate, Creatinine, Platelets, Hemoglobin.
    \item \texttt{radiology}: report text; \texttt{respiratory}: ventilation parameters.
    \item \texttt{risk\_scores}: mortality\_risk, sepsis\_risk, icu\_deterioration\_risk.
    \item \texttt{narrative}: formatted clinical summary string.
    \item \texttt{data\_source}: \texttt{eicu} or \texttt{synthetic}; \texttt{eicu\_stay\_id} when loaded from the demo database.
\end{itemize}
Temporal runs add \texttt{reasoning\_trace} (per-hour severity and reason), \texttt{respiratory\_escalation}, \texttt{sepsis\_escalation}, \texttt{coordinator\_summary}, and \texttt{temporal\_narrative}. Artifacts are written to \texttt{output/json}, \texttt{output/reports}, and \texttt{output/timelines}.

\section{Evaluation Metrics}

Evaluation focuses on pipeline correctness, eICU mapping fidelity, and clinical plausibility of rule-based reasoning:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Structural validity:} JSON bundles conform to expected keys; Pydantic validation on \texttt{PatientState}.
    \item \textbf{eICU mapping:} \texttt{patientunitstayid} resolves in SQLite; vitals and labs populate non-empty fields for cohort stays with measurements.
    \item \textbf{Risk monotonicity:} On held-out synthetic cases, higher lactate and lower SpO$_2$ increase mortality risk; on eICU stays, scores reflect measured lactate and latest SpO$_2$.
    \item \textbf{Temporal severity:} Critical hours in \texttt{reasoning\_trace} align with SpO$_2$/HR threshold breaches on eICU-derived traces.
    \item \textbf{Branching correctness:} Hypoxia branch fires when terminal SpO$_2$ $<$ 85; sepsis branch when lactate $>$ 4~mmol/L.
    \item \textbf{Operational metrics:} DAG success rate, artifact creation under \texttt{output/}, dashboard metadata (\texttt{data\_source}, \texttt{eicu\_stay\_id}).
\end{itemize}
Future work may add distributional comparison to published ICU statistics and LLM-judged narrative quality.

\chapter{Results and Discussion}

The pipeline was exercised through CLI runs, Streamlit sessions, and repeated Airflow DAG triggers. Each successful run produces a unique \texttt{patient\_id} and persisted artifacts.

\section{Result Analysis}

\textbf{Single-patient generation (eICU).} Running \texttt{python app.py --source eicu --stay-id <id>} loads a real demo stay, maps vitals/labs from SQLite, and prints the narrative. Example stay \texttt{147784} yields measured WBC, lactate, creatinine, FiO$_2$ from charting, and a 24-point vitals trace for reasoning.

\textbf{Multi-agent Airflow DAG.} With \texttt{ICU\_DATA\_SOURCE=eicu} and the demo volume mounted in Docker, \texttt{icu\_multi\_agent\_pipeline} writes bundles tagged \texttt{data\_source: eicu} and refreshes \texttt{latest\_dashboard.json} with \texttt{eicu\_stay\_id}, mortality risk, and latest SpO$_2$.

\textbf{Temporal reasoning DAG.} The \texttt{icu\_temporal\_reasoning\_pipeline} builds \texttt{reasoning\_trace} from eICU \texttt{vitalperiodic} rows, applies hypoxia/sepsis branches on measured SpO$_2$ and lactate, and stores coordinator escalation metadata plus \texttt{temporal\_narrative} under \texttt{output/timelines/}.

\textbf{Streamlit UI.} Operators select \texttt{patientunitstayid} from the demo cohort or switch to synthetic mode for regression testing.

\textbf{Discussion.} Grounding agents in eICU-CRD demo data improves external validity over pure simulation while retaining interpretable rule-based reasoning traces. Limitations include sparse temperature/SpO$_2$ in some stays, simplified risk scoring (not calibrated to APACHE/SOFA), and placeholder LangGraph integration. The hybrid design (eICU first, synthetic fallback) supports reproducible Airflow runs and future LLM enrichment on real clinical text from \texttt{note} tables.

\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}

\section{Conclusion}

This project delivered a framework for \textbf{generative clinical narrative and risk explanation} from ICU time-series data, integrating the PhysioNet eICU-CRD demo with the \texttt{icu\_agents} multi-agent stack. SQLite-backed loading, stay-level orchestration, and Airflow DAGs with explicit temporal reasoning traces transform structured measurements into human-readable reports and escalation-aware narratives. The work establishes a foundation for LLM-enhanced ICU decision support and aligns with the M.Tech project goals of improving interpretability through natural-language clinical summaries grounded in real critical-care data.

\section{Future Work}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Complete BeeAI agent workflow integration and Agent Stack deployment.
    \item Expand OpenAI LLM usage for insights and natural-language querying over eICU cohorts.
    \item Evaluate narratives against clinician review and published LLM summarization benchmarks.
    \item Calibrate risk scores against SOFA/APACHE using full eICU-CRD access.
    \item Extend cohort analytics (EDA, feature stores) on top of the integrated eICU loader already shared with agents and Airflow.
    \item Add FHIR export and monitoring for Airflow and agent pipeline health.
\end{itemize}
